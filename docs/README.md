OctoAcme Project Management Overview

OctoAcme runs projects using a lightweight, document-driven process that starts with a Project One-pager to validate the business need, define measurable success metrics, and align stakeholders. Work moves into planning only after success metrics, priority, and team availability are confirmed. Planning converts approved initiatives into a prioritized backlog with clear acceptance criteria, estimates, and a Definition of Done, producing a release plan and iteration backlog that guide execution.

Day-to-day execution is tracked on a project board with columns such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done. Pull requests follow a disciplined workflow—keeping changes small when possible, including issue links and acceptance criteria, running CI (tests, linting, and security scans) before requesting review, and requiring at least one approval before merging. Cross-team dependencies and risks are captured in the backlog and a simple risk register, and are surfaced during daily standups and weekly delivery syncs for timely escalation.

Roles and responsibilities are explicit: Product Managers define outcomes and prioritize the roadmap; Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and stakeholder communications; Developers implement and test features; QA validates acceptance criteria and quality. These role definitions help ensure clear ownership of artifacts like the Project One-pager, roadmap, acceptance criteria, and the risk register, and they guide who runs kickoffs, planning sessions, demos, and retrospectives.

Quality assurance and continuous improvement are integrated into the lifecycle. QA practices include unit and integration tests, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI security scans, and manual QA as needed. Releases follow pre-release checklists (including staging smoke tests and rollback plans), and retrospectives after sprints or incidents produce prioritized action items that are tracked back into the backlog. Escalation paths are defined (team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor) so issues with business impact can be addressed quickly and transparently.
